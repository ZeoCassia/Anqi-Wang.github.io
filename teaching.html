Introduction to the History of Western Philosophy

Level 1 · 2023–2024 and 2024–2025, Autumn

This Level 1 module introduces students to the development of Western philosophy as a distinctive mode of philosophical inquiry through the ideas of thinkers who have shaped its history. It covers philosophers from ancient Greece to the eighteenth century and examines their major ideas and contributions. Topics include, among others, Plato's Theory of Forms, Confucius's concept of li (ritual), Descartes' dualism, and the work of modern and contemporary women philosophers and feminist philosophy.

How Science Works

Level 1 · 2023–2024 and 2024–2025, Spring

This Level 1 module introduces students to the major stages of scientific practice, from discovery to justification, through a selection of central issues in the philosophy of science. Topics typically include theory-laden observation, scientific realism, the problem of induction, the value-free ideal of science, Kuhn and scientific revolutions, and related debates.

Philosophy of Biology

Levels 2 & 3 · 2024–2025, Spring

This Level 2 and Level 3 module examines a range of puzzles in the ontology, epistemology, and metaphysics of biology. It encourages students to think critically about how biological knowledge is developed and about the philosophical implications of that knowledge. Topics include the metaphysics of pregnancy, scientific models, the evolution of morality, natural selection and organismal traits, the ontology of disease, and biological natural kinds.

Module Design

Evolution of Morality

Philosophy of Biology, March 2025

This module examines how evolutionary processes may explain the origins of morality and what such explanations imply for moral knowledge and justification. It begins with the evolutionary puzzle of biological altruism and explores two major evolutionary explanations: kin selection and reciprocal altruism. It also introduces students to the concepts of the selfish gene and game theory. The module then turns to the philosophical implications of evolutionary debunking arguments, which challenge whether our evolved moral beliefs can justify objective moral truths.

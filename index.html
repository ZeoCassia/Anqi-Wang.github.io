About Me

My name is Anqi (ahn-chee) Wang (wahng). I am a PhD student in Philosophy at the University of Leeds, supervised by Juha Saatsi and Helen Beebee.

I am interested in philosophy of science broadly construed. My primary research interests lie in interventionism, causal modelling, and scientific explanation. I was drawn to The Book of Why: The New Science of Cause and Effect during my MA, which led me to begin studying causation and structural equation models (SEMs). During my PhD, my research has focused on causal explanation based on SEMs.

My thesis examines the problem of variable choice in SEM-based explanations. More specifically, it aims to identify and examine criteria that can guide us in selecting the best explanation when multiple SEM-based explanations of a phenomenon are available, each of which correctly represents certain causal dependencies.

For me, the study of causation and causal explanation serves as a crossroads for exploring broader topics in the philosophy of science. My research interests extend to areas including scientific models, representation, statistics, and explainable artificial intelligence, all of which I find deeply engaging.

Beyond academia, I have two passions that have consistently shaped my life. One is Japanese anime and creating J-anime-style illustrations. I have been working as an illustrator for ten years and thoroughly enjoy it. The other is photography. If I suddenly pull out my phone while walking or chatting, it is almost certainly because a bounding rabbit or a flying pigeon has prompted me to press the shutter.
